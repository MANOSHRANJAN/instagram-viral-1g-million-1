
Head of INSTAGRAM reveals secrets to GROWING in 2026

This video features Brock Johnson interviewing Adam Mosseri, the Head of Instagram, to discuss the future of the platform for creators in 2025 and 2026. The conversation provides an unfiltered look at how the algorithm works, the importance of various metrics, and what creators should prioritize for growth.

Key Takeaways and Algorithm Insights:

Top Creator Priorities (3:03 - 5:08): Instagram's main focus is helping smaller creators break out, rewarding original content, and ensuring that trends gain traction quickly.
Recommendations & Reach (5:08 - 8:01): The shift toward more "unconnected reach" (content from non-followers) was a strategic move to grow the platform and maintain the Feed as a relevant space for users.
Algorithm Dynamics (8:01 - 9:49): The algorithm receives subtle updates daily. Mosseri advises creators to look for long-term patterns rather than overreacting to the performance of a single post, as daily fluctuations are often influenced by real-world events or seasonality.
Essential Metrics (16:17 - 21:14): Shares are highly valued because they represent a human-to-human connection. Watch time (total time spent) is tracked rather than just watch rate (completion percentage), though the platform avoids optimizing solely for time to keep the experience balanced between entertainment and social connection.
Hashtags (21:14 - 23:36): Mosseri clarifies that hashtags are not a primary tool for increasing reach, and they will likely remain on the platform without plans for full removal, though their role has diminished.
Shadowbanning (27:36 - 29:53): The concept of "shadowbanning" is refuted; instead, Instagram has "Recommendation Guidelines." Users can check their Account Status in settings to see if their content is eligible for recommendation.
Future Features & Tools:

SEO & Content Search (11:05 - 16:17): Instagram is investing heavily in improving content-based search. Future updates will incorporate context from comments to help users find relevant content more easily.
Blends (34:51 - 38:11): This upcoming feature allows users to create a shared Reels feed within a direct message thread, fostering closer connections between friends.
Partner Ads (46:27 - 48:09): Mosseri expresses excitement about the long-term potential of Partner Ads, which allow for better measurement and monetization, helping to shift more value to smaller creators.


The Secret to the Algorithm

This video by Brock Johnson explores the importance of shares as the primary driver for Instagram virality and provides a strategic framework for creating shareworthy content.

Why Shares Matter (0:00 - 1:39)
Shares (sends per reach) are the most powerful metric for the Instagram algorithm.
While likes boost content for existing followers, high share rates push posts to non-followers, which is the key to going viral.
An Insta Club study suggests one share can generate 150 to 450 extra views.
The SHARE Framework (1:39 - 12:29)
Brock introduces the SHARE acronym to guide content creation:

S - Simple: Keep concepts easy to understand (the "drunk grandma" rule).
H - Hook-driven: Use 5–12 word hooks, simple language, and negative framing to stop the scroll.
A - Authentic: Reduce the time between idea and execution to maintain raw, relatable energy.
R - Relatable: Use specific details (e.g., instead of "I like ice cream," describe a specific scenario) to build connection.
E - Entertaining: While educational content gets saves, it needs to be entertaining to get shares.
5 Types of Shareworthy Content (12:29 - 22:00)
Motivational/Inspirational: Quotes or reminders that offer value over vanity.
Call-to-Arms: Content that rallies a community around a shared goal or cause.
Relatable Humor: Memes or skits that allow the sharer to look funny or understood.
Controversial Content: Sharing unpopular opinions or "busting myths" to allow others to align with your stance silently.
Timely/Newsworthy: Commentary on current events, trends, or platform updates.
Strategic Tips for Virality (22:00 - 23:42)
Success Leaves Clues: Study what is going viral in and outside of your niche and adapt those proven concepts.
Ask for the Share: This is an underused tactic. Explicitly tell your audience to share or send the post to someone who needs to see it.

In this video, Alex Hormozi outlines his 2026 social media strategy and the SPCL framework he uses to build genuine influence rather than just chasing views. He emphasizes that the goal for business owners should be to act as educators who attract their ideal avatar by focusing on interest-based content.

The SPCL Framework
Hormozi defines influence as a "high likelihood of compliance with requests" and breaks down the four elements needed to maximize it:

Status (0:46 - 3:08): Controlling scarce resources that others want.
Power (3:09 - 4:45): Establishing "say-do correspondence"—when you give advice and it produces a positive, observable result for the follower.
Credibility (4:46 - 4:51, 11:54 - 12:20): Utilizing third-party validation or objective proof (like Guinness World Records or business performance metrics) to build trust.
Likeness (4:52 - 7:19, 12:21 - 13:00): Being authentic and relatable to your specific audience; people follow those who share their values or resonate with them personally.
Strategic Shifts for 2026
Live and Interactive Content (12:43 - 16:32): Hormozi identifies a clear hierarchy in audience engagement: A-list celebrities (lowest engagement), Shorts (slight), Long-form (high), and Live Streamers (the highest engagement). He plans to shift his strategy toward live, unscripted, and interactive content to better approximate "real life" interactions.
Volume and Consistency (17:47 - 18:59): He argues that businesses must increase their content volume to see proportional growth in prospects, noting his own production of 35,000 pieces of content this year.
Interest Media over Social Media (19:08 - 21:09): He advises creators to stop optimizing for broad viral views and instead target the specific interests of their ideal customer avatar. The content itself acts as the targeting mechanism.
Reality Check on Market Size (21:58 - 24:36)
Hormozi encourages creators to have realistic expectations regarding their audience size. Using US census data, he explains that businesses aiming for specific niches (like high-revenue business owners) shouldn't compare themselves to general entertainers, as they are competing for a much smaller, more specific segment of the population.
