# Brock Johnson's IG Virality Framework

## His core "how to go viral every time" thesis

Brock's central thesis is that **shares are the single most important metric on Instagram**, and the secret to going viral every time is to make content that is "share-worthy." He argues: "for the last few years there has been one top dog metric, one best form of engagement that has been the best indicator or predictor of a post virality, and that is the number of shares that the post gets." Likes push your post to existing followers, but shares push it to non-followers — that's how virality happens. He cites an InstaClubHub study: "getting one additional comment on your post will equate to about 15 extra views. Getting one save will equate to about 45 extra views. Getting one like will equal about 75 extra views, and getting just one share on your post on average will equate to about 150 extra views, with some people getting 300 to 450 extra views per share." Recipients of shared posts are also "arguably the best possible viewer" because a friend, not the algorithm, has hand-picked them. His full operating framework is the SHARE acronym: **S**imple, **H**ook, **A**uthentic, **R**elatable, **E**ntertaining.

## Specific tactical rules he teaches

- **Optimize for shares above everything else.** "If your post is getting a lot more shares, it's getting a high share rate, then your post will be pushed out to more non followers, and that's how you go viral."
- **Make it Simple — pass the "drunk grandma rule."** "If your marketing wouldn't make sense to a drunk grandma, then it's probably too complex... your goal should be to make it so simple and easy to understand that even a grandma who's had a few glasses of wine could understand what the post was about." Use "simpler language… dumbed-down vocab… phrases and expressions that are easier to understand."
- **Hook hard in the first few seconds.** "The first metric that the algorithm checks is skip rate... they have to at least make it through the first few seconds or at least make it through the first one to two slides of your carousel."
- **Cut every two seconds.** "I recommend having some sort of cut in your video every two seconds... that's actually the rule that almost every TV show and movie sticks to. So by simply recording a new clip, changing your camera angle, or zooming in, you'll be able to hold people's attention for longer."
- **Be Authentic by reducing time between idea and execution.** "The secret to being authentic is reducing the time between idea and execution. Basically, the less time that you can spend editing and refining and perfecting and filtering and ChatGPTing your post, the more authentic it will be."
- **Say the quiet part out loud.** "Authentic posts are also the posts that say what that little voice in your head is saying. That little voice that no one else is acknowledging... When you post the quiet part out loud, people are like, 'Yes, finally someone was authentic enough to say it.'"
- **Get Relatable through specificity, not vagueness.** "Relatability comes from specificity... the more specific you can be, the more relatable your posts will be." (His vanilla-ice-cream-on-a-93-degree-day-blasting-Taylor-Swift example).
- **Be Entertaining first, educational second.** "Educational content can go viral and it can be share-worthy, but if and only if it is entertaining."
- **Repost everything three times.** "If you posted a reel more than 60 days ago, post it again today. And 60 days from now, post it a third time... Most of your followers probably didn't see it, and the few who did don't remember. So stop making new posts, stop looking for new ideas, and stop spending hours editing."
- **Value matters more than vanity.** "The actual message that you're sharing... is so much more important than how aesthetically pleasing it is or what fonts you use or what color background you use."
- **Keep posts on-niche even when borrowing outside material.** "I wouldn't, for example, use a David Goggins quote about running ultramarathons because my followers aren't into that sort of thing."
- **Steal proven formats — success leaves clues.** "Study the most shared and the most viral content in your niche... and also share what is going viral outside of your niche... it's going to be very share-worthy because it's using a proven concept."
- **Always ask for the share.** "Tell people to share your post. Don't just make a share-worthy post and then cross your fingers and pray to Adam Mosseri... Send this to a friend who needs to hear it. Help spread this news. Tell people to share and they will be more likely to do so."
- **Use the five share-worthy content buckets:** motivational/inspirational, community-oriented (call-to-arms), relatable humor/memes, controversial (unpopular opinions), and timely (news, commentary, pop culture, holidays).
- **Post call-to-arms reels sparingly but reliably.** "If you do one of these maybe once a month or even a few times a quarter, you can really hit it big because when these posts hit, they catch on like wildfire."

## Specific anti-rules — what he warns AGAINST

- **Don't post purely educational content.** "Purely educational content is rarely share-worthy. The only time where educational content is share-worthy is if it's really profound education or education teaching something that is like totally contrary to the norm."
- **Don't water content down to broaden appeal.** "There's a difference between simple and easy to understand versus broad and vague and watered down." Vague is not the same as simple.
- **Don't over-edit, over-filter, or over-ChatGPT your posts.** Doing so kills authenticity. "The less time that you can spend editing and refining and perfecting and filtering and ChatGPTing your post, the more authentic it will be."
- **Don't obsess over aesthetics.** "Value matters more than vanity." His example: Meredith Van Ness with 200K followers using "just a white background with a plain black font."
- **Don't constantly chase new ideas — repost old work first.** "Stop making new posts, stop looking for new ideas, and stop spending hours editing. You've already done the work, so post it again."
- **Don't be controversial if you can't take heat.** "If you're someone who really can't handle even just one negative comment, then stay away from controversial content."
- **Don't attack people for the sake of controversy.** "I'm not encouraging you to get canceled or to say anything defamatory or start cussing people out... I'm not encouraging you to attack others. What I am encouraging you to do is to post your unpopular opinions."
- **Don't just hope people share — that's lazy.** "Don't just make a share-worthy post and then cross your fingers and pray to Adam Mosseri."
- **Don't take "just be yourself" as authenticity advice.** "The secret to being authentic is to just be yourself. No, that's horrible advice."

## What he says about hooks

The H in SHARE stands for Hook, and Brock gives a specific formula for what makes a share-worthy hook:

- **Length: 5 to 12 words.** "The keys to a good share-worthy hook is that generally speaking, it's between 5 to 12 words long."
- **Use simple language.** "It uses simple language."
- **Use negative words.** "It uses negative words if possible, words like don't, stop, avoid, stay away from."
- **Pair with an easy-to-read visual.** "It has a visual that's very easy to see and thus easy to understand."
- **Catch them off guard.** His "Post everything three times" hook worked because "that hook was simple. It was three words and it kind of caught you off guard."
- **The hook is gatekeeper #1.** "The first metric that the algorithm checks is skip rate. And obviously, people aren't going to share your post if they aren't first stopping to at least watch your post."

## What he says about retention

Brock treats retention primarily as a function of pacing and the skip-rate threshold:

- **Cut every two seconds.** "We all know that the human attention span is short, but on Instagram, it's super short. So I recommend having some sort of cut in your video every two seconds."
- **Match the cadence of TV/film.** "That's actually the rule that almost every TV show and movie sticks to."
- **Cuts can be camera angle changes or zooms — not just new clips.** "By simply recording a new clip, changing your camera angle, or zooming in, you'll be able to hold people's attention for longer and increase your engagement."
- **Skip rate is the first algorithmic gate.** "The first thing that the algorithm checks is your post skip rate, or basically are people actually hooked and going to be watching the video. But then once it crosses that threshold, the algorithm is looking at two main indicators, the number of likes and the number of shares."
- **Full-watch isn't strictly required to win.** "You don't have to have them watch the entire thing. If you do, that's great. But they have to at least make it through the first few seconds."

## What he says about reels vs other formats

- **Reels are the engine but carousels still play if hooked properly.** "They have to at least make it through the first few seconds or at least make it through the first one to two slides of your carousel."
- **DMs are where IG attention now lives, not feed.** "Instagram has figured out that friends and family and the average Instagram user isn't posting things on their feed anymore. So instead, they're engaging with Instagram and they're hooked on Instagram because of what's going on in the direct messages."
- **Build content for DM-shareability specifically.** "If you can create a post that is worthy of being sent from one person to another via direct message, via text message, or via any other method, then Instagram will reward you for doing so."
- **Repost the same reel on a 60-day cadence.** "If you posted a reel more than 60 days ago, post it again today. And 60 days from now, post it a third time."
- **Format choices (length, aspect ratio, talking-head vs. trending audio) matter less than share-worthiness.** His "triplets" reel that listed conflicting format rules was deliberately his lowest-performing example because format debates aren't relatable, authentic, or hook-driven.

## Where he disagrees with mainstream creator advice

- **"Just be yourself" is bad authenticity advice.** "Now, the secret to being authentic is to just be yourself. No, that's horrible advice." He replaces it with: minimize the time between idea and execution.
- **Likes are overrated as a virality metric.** Most creators chase likes, but "if your post is getting a high amount of likes, then your post will be pushed out to more of your existing followers" — that doesn't grow you. Shares do.
- **Educational content is overhyped.** "Purely educational content is rarely share-worthy." Most creators lean educational; he says lean entertaining.
- **Vague/broad content does NOT travel further — specific does.** Counter to the instinct to water down so "everyone gets it."
- **Aesthetics are overrated.** "Value matters more than vanity." Pretty fonts and color palettes are not what makes things share.
- **Stop chasing new ideas; repost.** Most advice is "post fresh content daily" — Brock says repost what already worked, on a 60-day rotation, for two more turns.
- **Don't trust the algorithm to find your right viewers.** "The algorithm is constantly showing me things that I'm not all that into. It's experimenting. But my wife also sends me posts all day, and every single post that my wife sends me gets watched... she knows me better than the algorithm."
- **Format-rule debates (7-second reels vs. 15, 4:5 vs. 3:4, talking-head vs. trending audio) are a distraction.** His own example of this kind of post was his worst-performing.
