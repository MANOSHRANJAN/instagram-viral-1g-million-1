# Hormozi's 2026 Social Media Strategy

> Source: single transcript where Hormozi lays out his SPCL framework and announces his shift to "live interactive" content (Mosy Media 3.0 / ACQ 3.0). Where the transcript is silent on a section below, that is called out explicitly rather than invented.

---

## What Hormozi says is DIFFERENT in 2026 vs prior years

- **He is moving off pre-recorded YouTube videos and going all-in on live streams.** Direct quote: *"I honestly hate making YouTube videos. What I mean that is like staring at a camera and having like prompts to solicit me to say stuff... I'm going all in on this. So if you guys are like, what's Alex's media strategy for the future? I'm focusing on two words. You can write this down. **Live interactive.** Those are the two things... that is describing the ACQ 3.0 or Mosy Media 3.0 vision for what's going forward."*
- **Social media is now "interest media."** *"Social media is now turning into interest media."* The algorithm is now so accurate that **the content itself is the targeting**: *"The content is the targeting. The algorithm is so good now. It knows what you're talking about. It can literally judge your background. It judges what you're wearing. It judges who you are and will display it to the people that they know have a history of watching content that is similar."*
- **The internet is moving toward "truth" / rawness.** A-listers (scripted, polished, photoshopped) get the least applause; rawer formats (shorts → longs → live) get progressively more. *"I think that the internet will always move towards truth."*
- **The hierarchy of influence has reordered.** Based on the Mr. Beast UK-vs-US celebrity soccer game story, the applause meter went: A-listers (almost none) → Shorts creators (slight) → Long-form podcasters/YouTubers (legit) → **Live streamers (the entire stadium erupted).**
- **Views are no longer the metric.** Chasing views is "dumb" if you sell services. The correct goal is **likelihood of compliance with a future request.**
- **He references the "podcast election"** (Trump-Rogan 3hr, Musk-Tucker) as proof that long, raw, multi-hour exposure now moves outcomes more than short polished media.

## Platform priorities

1. **Live interactive (top priority going forward)** — "ACQ 3.0 / Mosy Media 3.0"
2. **Long-form** (podcasts, long YouTube) — high reinforcement cycles, builds real influence
3. **Shorts** — kept as a top-of-funnel feeder: *"I see the purpose of shorts as many times a way to get someone to watch a long. They watch a couple shorts and then think, okay, this guy seems legit, I'm going to risk my time."*

Volume reference points he cites: **32.7M YouTube views in the month**, **35,000 pieces of content this year**, **$100M+ book launch in 72 hours.**

## His content production system

The transcript does not walk through a step-by-step idea→shoot→edit→post workflow or name tools/roles. What it does state operationally:

- **Volume is the input-output machine.** *"How do I just jam as much into that input output machine as I possibly can? And as long as I'm checking these boxes... then you're going to get the right kind of prospects."*
- **35,000 pieces/year vs the typical operator's 365.** *"We're just quite literally doing 100 times more. And as a result of that 100 times the volume, what do you think's happening? We get 100 times the prospect."*
- **Outlier math is linear and predictable.** *"We just know that one out of 10 shorts is going to be a two or three outlier. We just know what that math looks like. Same thing for longs."*
- **Live streaming is the new production primitive** because it is unscripted and uncuttable: *"streaming, it's like, yeah, we're live, right? I can't do anything... How can we approximate the rawest reality of you us hanging out."*
- A repeated format he calls out by name: **"cash cows"** — a business owner presents their business and Hormozi works on it with them on camera.

## His current hook playbook

The transcript does not give named hook formulas, but it shows the hook he opened this video with and explains it as **a Status hook**:

- **Open with proof of controlling a scarce resource.** *"At the very beginning... I said we did 32.7 million views and we did over 100 million in sales for the book launch in 72 hours. And so that's me demonstrating status."*
- **Pair the status flex with a Power promise** — tell them what they will be able to do if they comply: *"What I'm going to do in this video is I'm going to break down four things. So you can follow and if you follow these things, you're going to be more likely to get people going to comply with your future requests."*
- **Implication for graders:** a strong Hormozi-style opener stacks (a) a number/proof-of-result + (b) a named framework + (c) a specific outcome the viewer gets if they stay.

## His current retention playbook

Not laid out as a numbered formula in the transcript. What he does say:

- **Maximize "reinforcement cycles"** — every say-do-good-thing-happens loop the viewer experiences with you increases compliance with the next request. Long form has more cycles than short form by orders of magnitude (his math: 480 fifteen-second shorts ≈ two 1-hour videos).
- **Stack SPCL throughout the body**, not just at the hook: status (you control scarce resources), power (you give directions that work), credibility (third-party proof — e.g., he paid Guinness judges on-site to validate the book launch records), likeness (just be you).
- **Use observable, operational language**, not emotional/abstract language. *"I like to look at objective reality and describe how you would see it with your eyes rather than try and put a whole bunch of emotional words around stuff."*
- **Defend why you do what you do.** *"What makes you unique is if you actually lean into the nuances that make you you and actually have a way to defend why you do what you do."*

## His current distribution play

The transcript does not discuss saves vs shares vs sends as algorithmic levers. What he optimizes for instead:

- **IRL response, not views.** *"I care so much more about IRL responses... If I make a video and then I get texts from business owners that I like and that I respect being like, yo, that was fire, then I'm like, okay, I'm on the right track."*
- **Right-prospect over high-view.** *"You absolutely can make plenty of money with a very small following as long as you make content that's directly valuable for that following."* He cites two businesses doing >$1M/yr with <5,000 followers.
- **Calibrate expectations to the addressable market.** His math: 9% of the US owns a business (~32M); 5% of those are >$1M (~1.5M); ~1 in 3,000 hit $100M. So 100k views on a video targeted at $1M+ owners is "crushing it" — comparing yourself to Mr. Beast is a category error.

## His current conversion play

- **CTAs are framed as compliance asks of varying "DnD roll difficulty."** *"There's levels of how big of an ask something is... Like or subscribe, it doesn't matter. You make some sort of solicitation."* Stack more SPCL → roll a lower number to succeed → bigger asks become viable.
- **Stack proof on every asset.** Why his ads work: *"The reason that my ads do well when I have my $10 million building behind me is like, oh, well, that's hard to fake. And so you have credibility there."*
- **Single events should multi-task across SPCL.** The book launch gave him status (revenue), credibility (Guinness validation), and power (he can now teach others to launch and they get results).
- **Named funnel asset in the transcript:** Skool ($n/month after 14-day trial) — placed below the video as the entry-level offer.

## His take on personal brand vs business brand

**The transcript does not directly address running a personal page and a business page in parallel.** What is present and adjacent:

- He references **Leila** running her own brand on overlapping topics (money/business) but pulling a 54% female audience vs his 89% male — implying the *person* drives the audience composition more than the *topic*, so two pages with the same subject matter can cleanly serve different segments.
- He distinguishes **educator vs entertainer**: *"If you sell services to anyone, you're likely going to be an educator, not an entertainer."* Both pages should be educator-mode if both are commerce-attached.
- Brand-level proof (e.g., the Guinness-validated $100M launch, the $10M building backdrop) is treated as portable across his content — implying business-brand assets should be deliberately created so they can be reused on personal-brand content for credibility.

If you grade scripts against this section, treat it as **inferred, not stated**.

## Specific tactical rules (rapid-fire)

**Do:**
- Stack all four of S-P-C-L in every piece, not just one.
- Open with status proof (a hard-to-fake number or artifact).
- Make a power promise — tell them what good thing happens if they follow your direction.
- Bring in third-party validators (Guinness, named institutions, on-camera receipts) for credibility.
- Lean into your actual self for likeness; defend why you do what you do.
- Use observable, operational language ("here is what you can see with your eyes"), not abstract emotional language.
- Treat shorts as feeders to longs; treat longs as feeders to live.
- Maximize total minutes of exposure with each prospect; prefer 2 hours over 480 shorts.
- Volume: aim for orders of magnitude more output than competitors (his ratio: 100×).
- Judge content by IRL responses from your real ICP, not by view count.
- Right-size expectations to your TAM (a piano-repair channel has a smaller ceiling than Mr. Beast — that is fine).
- Build proof assets (records, buildings, validators) once and reuse them across all content.

**Don't:**
- Don't chase views as the goal if you sell services.
- Don't compare your views to entertainers in different markets.
- Don't be an NPC — don't pick an archetype outfit/persona to imitate.
- Don't act in a way that is different from who you are ("zero ROI" in it).
- Don't over-script or over-polish — A-listers get the least applause.
- Don't think in binaries (have/don't have status); think in continuums.
- Don't make "social" content (engagement bait, grandma-slap virality) if your goal is the right prospect — make **interest** content.
- Don't try to outsmart the volume requirement: *"People want to try and outsmart themselves and thinking that they can not do the work that's required, but it's actually far more linear than you would expect."*

## Grading note for the agent

When scoring an Instagram reel against the Hormozi 20% weight, the load-bearing checks are:

1. **SPCL stacking** — does the script demonstrate Status, Power, Credibility, and Likeness, or only one/two?
2. **Operational/observable language** — is the proof something a viewer can see with their eyes, or is it abstract claims?
3. **Educator framing** — is it making the viewer more capable, or just entertaining?
4. **Compliance-shaped CTA** — is there a graduated ask that the SPCL stack has earned?
5. **Right-prospect not high-view** — is the content unmistakably aimed at a specific ICP, or is it general-interest bait?
6. **Truth/rawness signal** — is it over-polished/scripted, or does it feel live/real?
