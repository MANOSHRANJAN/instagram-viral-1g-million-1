## 3. The 3 pillars — how to talk about each without sounding scattered

The trick: don't present these as 3 separate services. Present them as **3 parts of ONE system** ("get found → get booked → never lose the lead"). That's the story that makes it feel cohesive, not random.

| Pillar | The story you tell | Proof to show |
|---|---|---|
| **Websites** | "A website that actually converts, not just looks nice" | Before/after site, load-time, booking-form conversion |
| **AI Ads** | "Ads that get you in front of the right person, cheap" | Screen-record of ad performance, cost-per-lead numbers |
| **AI Automation** | "An employee that never misses a call or a lead" | The AI receptionist demo — this is your strongest proof asset |

**Lead with automation.** Your dental AI-receptionist demo is your single best piece of proof (it's real, it's visual, it's the "wait, what?" hook). Websites and ads support it — "and once the leads come in, this is how we make sure none of them get lost."

## 5. What makes it feel "real" (not like an agency ad account)

- **Show your face consistently** — talking-head, same setup, same energy (this is what turns an account into a *person* people follow, not a business page they scroll past).
- **Show the actual builds** — screen-record the AI receptionist working, the ad dashboard, the website live. Proof > claims, every time.
- **Use real numbers from real clients** (with permission) — "47 missed calls → 31 booked" hits because it's specific, not "we help businesses grow."
- **Post the failures too, occasionally** — "this automation didn't work the first time, here's what I fixed" builds more trust than a highlight reel.
